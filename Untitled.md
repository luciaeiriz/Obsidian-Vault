

#### Experiment 3 — Mapping the relative-orbit Doppler ambiguity

##### Measurement-valid solutions

An optimiser termination flag is not treated as sufficient evidence of a valid solution.

A solution is classified as measurement-valid when

$$
\max_k
\left|
f_{D,k}^{pred}
-
f_{D,k}^{meas}
\right|
<
10^{-7}\ {\rm Hz}.
$$

This is a numerical exact-fit tolerance for the current noise-free synthetic problem.

Out of 1000 optimisation runs:

$$
898
$$

were measurement-valid.

The best rejected solution had a maximum Doppler residual of approximately

$$
131\ {\rm Hz}.
$$

The valid solutions have residuals of approximately

$$
10^{-10}\ {\rm Hz},
$$

so there is a very large separation between valid and rejected solutions.

---

##### Relative-state branches

The 898 measurement-valid runs collapsed into **four distinct relative-state branches**.

The representative relative states are:

**Branch 1**

$$
\Delta\mathbf r_0
=
[125.503,\,-3550.667,\,-2298.389]\ {\rm km},
$$

$$
\Delta\mathbf v_0
=
[4.475415,\ 0.875560,\,-0.562420]\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**Branch 2**

$$
\Delta\mathbf r_0
=
[-2559.820,\ 1775.010,\ 2863.955]\ {\rm km},
$$

$$
\Delta\mathbf v_0
=
[-3.346644,\,-0.524123,\,-3.104437]\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**Branch 3 — truth**

$$
\Delta\mathbf r_0
=
[-2559.820,\ 2512.218,\ 2245.364]\ {\rm km},
$$

$$
\Delta\mathbf v_0
=
[-3.346644,\,-2.966260,\,-1.055240]\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**Branch 4**

$$
\Delta\mathbf r_0
=
[125.503,\,-1646.905,\,-3895.835]\ {\rm km},
$$

$$
\Delta\mathbf v_0
=
[4.475415,\,-0.705915,\ 0.764595]\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

Branch 3 is the true relative state.

The four branches are separated by approximately

$$
962-8038\ {\rm km}
$$

in relative position and

$$
2.064-8.729\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}
$$

in relative velocity.

They therefore represent genuinely different relative orbits.

The cluster memberships are unchanged from the earlier absolute-B-state analysis.

This is expected because A is fixed and common to every solution:

$$
\Delta\mathbf r_j-\Delta\mathbf r_k
=
(\mathbf r_{B,j}-\mathbf r_A)
-
(\mathbf r_{B,k}-\mathbf r_A)
=
\mathbf r_{B,j}-\mathbf r_{B,k},
$$

and similarly

$$
\Delta\mathbf v_j-\Delta\mathbf v_k
=
\mathbf v_{B,j}-\mathbf v_{B,k}.
$$

Subtracting the same known A state therefore preserves all pairwise branch distances.

This confirms that the fourfold ambiguity is an ambiguity in the **relative state**, not merely in the absolute representation of B.

---

##### Do the four relative states produce the same measurements?

For every branch,

$$
\rho_j(t)
=
\|\Delta\mathbf r_j(t)\|.
$$

The maximum difference between any two branch range histories is approximately

$$
1.2\times10^{-10}\ {\rm km},
$$

consistent with numerical precision.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\rho_1(t)
\approx
\rho_2(t)
\approx
\rho_3(t)
\approx
\rho_4(t).
}
$$

All four relative states also have the same initial range,

$$
\rho_0
\approx
4231.498301\ {\rm km}.
$$

The equality is not restricted to $t=0$.

The complete range histories are identical to numerical precision.

The range-rate histories also satisfy

$$
\dot\rho_1(t)
\approx
\dot\rho_2(t)
\approx
\dot\rho_3(t)
\approx
\dot\rho_4(t).
$$

Since

$$
f_D(t)
=
-\frac{f_0}{c}\dot\rho(t),
$$

the Doppler histories are also identical.

The largest pairwise Doppler difference is approximately

$$
1.2\times10^{-10}\ {\rm Hz}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\text{different relative states}
\rightarrow
\text{same }\rho(t)
\rightarrow
\text{same }\dot\rho(t)
\rightarrow
\text{same }f_D(t).
}
$$

This is the key result of the experiment.

##### Geometrical explanation

The four branches can be explained by two symmetries of the circular, co-altitude geometry.

For the circular case define

$$
\mathbf u(t)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos nt\\
\sin nt\\
0
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where

$$
n
=
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a^3}}.
$$

The two circular trajectories can be written as

$$
\mathbf r_A(t)
=
aC_A\mathbf u(t),
$$

$$
\mathbf r_B(t)
=
aC_B\mathbf u(t),
$$

with a constant relative rotation

$$
R
=
C_A^TC_B.
$$

The angle between the two radius vectors is

$$
\cos\psi(t)
=
\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u.
$$

Since both satellites have radius $a$,

$$
\boxed{
\rho^2(t)
=
2a^2
\left[
1-\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u
\right].
}
$$

Therefore, transformations that preserve

$$
\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u
$$

also preserve the relative range history and hence the Doppler history.

---

###### Symmetry 1 — Transpose / inverse

Because

$$
\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u
$$

is a scalar,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u
=
\mathbf u^TR^T\mathbf u.
}
$$

Therefore

$$
R
\rightarrow
R^T=R^{-1}
$$

does not change the range history.

The recovered transpose pairings are

$$
1\leftrightarrow3
$$

and

$$
2\leftrightarrow4.
$$

---

###### Symmetry 2 — Reflection across A's orbital plane

In A's orbital frame, reflection across A's plane is represented by

$$
D
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&-1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Because A lies in this plane,

$$
D\mathbf u=\mathbf u.
$$

For

$$
R'
=
DRD,
$$

we obtain

$$
\mathbf u^TR'\mathbf u
=
\mathbf u^TDRD\mathbf u
=
\mathbf u^TR\mathbf u.
$$

Reflection therefore also preserves the complete range and Doppler histories.

The recovered reflection pairings are

$$
1\leftrightarrow4
$$

and

$$
2\leftrightarrow3.
$$

---

###### Why four branches?

For the present geometry, the two binary transformations generate

$$
R,
\qquad
R^T,
\qquad
DRD,
\qquad
DR^TD.
$$

The observed branches correspond to

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Branch 3} &\leftrightarrow R
&&\text{(truth)},\\
\text{Branch 1} &\leftrightarrow R^T,\\
\text{Branch 2} &\leftrightarrow DRD,\\
\text{Branch 4} &\leftrightarrow DR^TD.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

All four preserve the same range history and therefore the same Doppler history.

For this particular non-degenerate geometry,

$$
\boxed{
2\text{ transpose possibilities}
\times
2\text{ reflection possibilities}
=
4\text{ relative-state branches}.
}
$$

---

##### Conclusion

The systematic multi-start search recovers four isolated **relative-state** solution branches within the investigated parameter domain.

These branches are physically distinct:

$$
\mathbf x_{{\rm rel},1}
\neq
\mathbf x_{{\rm rel},2}
\neq
\mathbf x_{{\rm rel},3}
\neq
\mathbf x_{{\rm rel},4}.
$$

However, all four produce the same

$$
\rho(t),
\qquad
\dot\rho(t),
\qquad
f_D(t)
$$

to numerical precision.

The ambiguity is therefore not caused by optimiser failure and is not an artefact of analysing B's absolute state.

It is a genuine ambiguity in the **relative orbit** under the present circular, co-altitude geometry.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\text{perfect Doppler fit}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unique relative orbit}.
}
$$

The local Jacobian remains full rank around an individual branch, so the current problem can be locally identifiable while remaining globally non-unique:

$$
\boxed{
\text{local identifiability}
\neq
\text{global uniqueness}.
}
$$

The symmetry analysis explains the four recovered branches through transpose/inverse and orbital-plane-reflection transformations.

The finite multi-start search provides strong numerical evidence for these four branches in the investigated domain, but it does not by itself prove that no additional global solutions exist.

---

#### Baseline result before changing geometry

Experiments 1–3 establish the current reference case:

> Under the circular, same-radius, noise-free two-body geometry, a single inter-satellite Doppler time history does not uniquely determine the relative state. Four distinct relative-state branches were recovered numerically, and the observed branches are related by two discrete geometric symmetries.

The next stage is therefore to change the **geometry of the relative orbit** one feature at a time and determine whether the ambiguity persists.

Planned changes include:

- same plane but different orbital radius;
- eccentricity;
- observation arc length;
- relative inclination;
- RAAN / phase geometry;
- initial satellite separation.

No measurement noise or other engineering effects are introduced at this stage.
