
1. **Angle-only OD first**
    - started with angle-only OD as you suggested.
    - worked through a simplified derivation manually

2. **Then moved to Doppler**
    - moved to Doppler
    - I read the most relevant papers I could find and took structured notes rather than trying to read everything at once.
    - Al-Hourani was particularly useful because it gives a very simplified two-satellite Doppler geometry.
3. **Built the first Doppler model**
    - I re-derived the Doppler relation myself, but I wrote it directly in terms of relative position and velocity because I eventually want to estimate the relative state.
    - Then I created two circular co-altitude satellite orbits, generated the Doppler history from the truth, hid B's state, and used nonlinear least squares to recover B.
4. **First result: the estimator works**
    - With an initial estimate reasonably close to the truth, it recovered the true orbit essentially exactly.
5. **Then the interesting problem appeared**
    - tried much more widely separated initial estimates.
    - Some of them converged to completely different B states but with effectively zero Doppler residual.
    - realised that a perfect Doppler fit did not necessarily mean I had recovered the true orbit.
6. **Then I investigated whether this was systematic**
    - I ran 1000 different initial estimates.
    - 898 gave measurement-valid exact fits.
    - Those 898 solutions collapsed into four distinct Cartesian states.
    - So it wasn't just random optimiser behaviour.
7. **Then you tried to understand why**
    - I compared the full range histories, range-rate histories and Doppler histories.
    - All four branches have the same range history to numerical precision, so naturally they also have the same range-rate and Doppler.
    - I then found two geometric symmetries connecting the branches: reflection across A's orbital plane and transpose/inversion of the relative rotation.
8. **Where you are now**
    - So at the moment, the simple circular co-altitude case is locally recoverable but globally non-unique.
    - The next thing I want to do is relax one assumption at a time, starting with different orbital radii, and see what breaks the symmetry.
