### Persistent Notes
This paper derives an **analytical model for the Doppler shift produced by a LEO satellite as a function of time**. The setup is still satellite → ground terminal, and the purpose is primarily communications rather than orbit determination.

The most useful part for us is that they explicitly connect **orbital geometry → changing range → Doppler**.

There is also a small element of going **backwards from Doppler to geometry**. They show that the shape of the Doppler-time curve depends on the geometry of the satellite pass, particularly the **maximum elevation angle**. The S-curves on page 1 illustrate this. 

Then in Section III they develop an algorithm that uses **two Doppler measurements and two Doppler-rate measurements** to estimate parameters of the Doppler curve, including the time of zero Doppler and associated maximum elevation angle.

**They demonstrate that Doppler history contains information about relative geometry**

Their objective is to predict the Doppler curve so that the ground terminal can improve things.
